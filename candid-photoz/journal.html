Planning your day

Make a little room for the photographs

A useful wedding photography timeline begins with the actual ceremony and the people who need to be present. Share the event schedule with your photographer, makeup artist and planner early.

Keep a dedicated slot for couple portraits and list your family groups in advance. A family member who knows everyone can help gather people while the photographer stays focused on the pictures.

Ask your photographer about the available light at your venue, travel between locations, and time for outfit changes. Leave breathing room around the important rituals so the schedule does not become another source of stress.

Feeling comfortable

For the couple who feels camera-shy

You do not need to arrive with a collection of poses. A helpful first step is explaining what you enjoy and what makes you feel awkward in front of a camera.

Bring a few references and describe the feeling you like in each photograph. Walking together, sharing a conversation or taking a quiet moment can be easier than holding a fixed pose.

Meet or speak with the person who will lead your shoot. Ask how they guide couples and how they balance portraits with candid coverage. Comfort begins with knowing who will be there.

Choosing your team

What to ask before booking your photographer

A beautiful portfolio is a starting point. Your booking conversation should also cover the team, scope, files, delivery and what happens if the day changes.

Ask to see a complete wedding photographed in conditions similar to yours. Confirm the lead team, event-by-event working hours, backup arrangements, travel charges and payment stages.

Write down the edited image quantities, file formats, film lengths, revision allowances, album specifications and delivery milestones. A clear agreement gives everyone a shared understanding of the work.
