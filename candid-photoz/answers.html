Planning & answers

Find practical answers about coverage, preparation, deliverables and what to confirm in your own proposal.

40 questions

Do we need photography, films or both?

Photographs give you individual moments to print, share and put in an album. Films add voices, movement and sound. Start with what you would actually like to revisit, then decide which events need each kind of coverage.

For your proposal: Confirm the photo-and-film combination and crew for each event.

Discuss this question
What is the difference between candid and traditional photographs?

Candid coverage follows moments as they unfold. Directed portraits and traditional group photographs involve some guidance and arrangement. A celebration can include both: natural interactions alongside organised photographs with your important people.

For your proposal: Agree on the balance you prefer before the day.

Can a small or at-home wedding still have thoughtful coverage?

A smaller celebration can have meaningful family moments, portraits and rituals. Share your venue, approximate guest count and events so the coverage can be shaped around what is actually happening.

For your proposal: Availability, staffing and the quotation depend on your plan.

Can we see a complete wedding gallery?

A full gallery helps you judge consistency across preparation, family photographs, rituals and evening lighting. The photographs on this preview are curated highlights from different shoots, not one complete wedding.

For your proposal: Request a full gallery from a celebration similar to yours before booking.

Do we have to do a pre-wedding shoot?

It is optional. It can be a chance to spend time together, become familiar with the camera and create photographs for yourselves or your invitations. You can also focus your coverage on the wedding celebrations.

For your proposal: Ask how adding or removing a session changes the proposed coverage.

Can we enquire about pre-wedding photographs without a film?

Yes. The enquiry form lets you request photographs only, films only, or both. Selecting an option starts a discussion; it does not reserve a team or confirm a package.

For your proposal: The studio needs to confirm the available service combination and quotation.

How does guest count affect the team?

Think about simultaneous events and important people as well as the total guest count. Two preparation locations or overlapping rituals may need different coverage from a single intimate ceremony.

For your proposal: Ask for the crew and lead assigned to each event in writing.

What costs should we include beyond the studio’s quote?

Check venue or shoot fees, permissions, makeup and hair, outfits, props, crew travel and accommodation, local transport, additional hours, extra albums and taxes. Some may already be included or may not apply. Use the cost worksheet to record each once.

For your proposal: Get a written inclusion/exclusion list from the studio and each external supplier.

Does Silver include a pre-wedding shoot and a sangeet?

The original Silver artwork says pre-wedding OR sangeet. It should not be interpreted as both being included.

For your proposal: Confirm which event you are choosing, the hours included and the cost of any addition.

Does 100, 150 or 200 premium edited photos mean the entire gallery?

The published collections list those quantities as premium edited photographs. The artwork does not clearly establish whether these are the entire delivered gallery or a selected level of retouching.

For your proposal: Ask for total delivered JPEGs, specially retouched images and album selections as separate quantities.

What do the two albums in a collection mean?

The published Gold and Premium references list two albums. Their sizes and page counts are shown on the prices page. Identical copies, separate selections and separate designs are different products.

For your proposal: Confirm which version the studio includes and how extra pages or copies are charged.

How do payments and booking confirmation work?

Before paying, request a written proposal with the event schedule, deliverables, payment stages and what reserves the dates. The form on this website prepares an enquiry; it does not take a payment or confirm a booking.

For your proposal: The studio must supply its current payment schedule and confirmation process.

What happens if our wedding or shoot date changes?

Tell the studio as soon as the plan changes. Studio availability and venue or travel bookings may need to be revisited separately.

For your proposal: Ask for the written rescheduling, cancellation and refund terms before booking. No particular outcome is promised here.

How long should our pre-wedding session be?

Build the session around usable photography time. Travel, outfit changes, hair and makeup, entry checks and breaks all use part of the day. The schedule worksheet helps you see the trade-offs before choosing too many stops.

For your proposal: The final duration and coverage hours belong in your proposal.

How many outfits should we bring?

Choose a manageable number that you can move, sit and walk in comfortably. Each change also needs somewhere private and time for adjustments. Use the schedule worksheet to check whether your changes fit the session.

For your proposal: Confirm included outfit changes and changing arrangements for your chosen location.

Are makeup, hair, clothes and props included?

These are separate planning items unless your proposal explicitly includes them. Tell the studio whether you want help arranging them, then confirm who books and pays each supplier.

For your proposal: This preview does not promise rental outfits, a makeup artist or props.

We feel awkward in front of a camera. What should we do?

Tell the photographer what feels comfortable and share a few references you like. Walking, talking or spending time together can be an easier starting point than holding an unfamiliar pose. You can also explain any movements or closeness you prefer to avoid.

For your proposal: Discuss the lead photographer’s approach to direction before booking.

How should we choose a shoot location?

Start with the setting you connect with, then consider travel, walking, privacy, changing rooms, toilets, shade, permissions and a weather alternative. A beautiful location is only useful if the day works for both of you.

For your proposal: Check current access and facilities with the exact property or authority.

Does an entry ticket allow a professional photoshoot?

Do not assume so. A venue may have a separate professional photo/video booking, crew limit or charge. Our location guides link to official information where available.

For your proposal: Agree who arranges permission and obtain current venue approval before travelling.

What if it rains or the weather is uncomfortable?

Discuss an indoor alternative, a different time or a backup date while planning. The couple, studio and venue should agree which conditions would change the plan.

For your proposal: The studio’s written policy determines rescheduling costs and availability.

What time of day should we shoot?

The right time depends on the light, access hours, crowd levels, weather and the look you want. A plan can include morning, shaded daylight or evening scenes. Exact times need the date and location.

For your proposal: Confirm venue opening and the photographer’s lighting plan; no universal sunrise or sunset time is assumed.

How early should we do the pre-wedding shoot?

Work backwards from when you need the finished material. Invitations, a wedding website or a screening need time for the shoot, editing, any agreed revisions and your own final preparation.

For your proposal: Ask the studio to confirm that your required delivery date is achievable.

What should we carry on the shoot?

Bring your agreed outfits, comfortable footwear for walking, small touch-up items, water, snacks and any personal props you have discussed. Check changing arrangements and leave time for breaks.

For your proposal: Use the preparation checklist and adapt it to the venue, weather and your own needs.

Can we discuss walking, access or privacy needs?

Yes. Share practical requirements such as fewer stairs, short transfers, a private changing space, regular breaks or a quieter setting. You do not need to explain private medical details in an enquiry.

For your proposal: Confirm the actual venue facilities before choosing the route.

Who will actually photograph and film our wedding?

Ask to meet or speak with the lead people proposed for your celebration, and see work relevant to the events they will cover. The person answering an enquiry may not be the person assigned to every event.

For your proposal: Names, roles, crew per event and any substitution process need studio confirmation.

How can we plan photographs with our parents and family?

Make a short prioritised group list and choose someone who knows the family to help gather people. Share important combinations and any timing constraints before the wedding. Our family-photo worksheet can help.

For your proposal: The plan improves coordination; it cannot guarantee every photograph regardless of timing or attendance.

Can you cover the bride and groom getting ready separately?

Share both locations and their schedules. Overlapping preparation and travel between venues affects how the crew should be arranged.

For your proposal: Confirm who covers each location and whether an additional photographer or filmmaker is needed.

What happens if an event runs late?

Build some breathing room into the schedule and agree a family or planner contact for changes. Coverage hours and overtime should be clear before the event.

For your proposal: Ask when the coverage clock starts, when it ends and how extra hours are approved and charged.

What backup arrangements should we discuss?

Ask separately about spare equipment, a lead-team absence, how files are copied after the event and how long delivered material is retained. These are different parts of the backup plan.

For your proposal: The studio must confirm its actual process; this preview does not assert a particular backup system.

How should photographers, filmmakers and content creators work together?

Share the full vendor list and decide who coordinates timings and key positions. Family members can also help keep important sightlines clear during rituals.

For your proposal: Confirm whether the studio has any exclusivity or coordination conditions before hiring additional teams.

What is the difference between a reel, teaser, film and ceremony recording?

A reel is a short social-format edit. A teaser introduces the celebration. A highlight or cinematic film selects moments into a story. A ceremony recording aims to document agreed rituals continuously. One does not automatically include the others.

For your proposal: Confirm duration, quantity, orientation and full-ceremony or performance coverage in writing.

Discuss this question
When will our photographs, films and albums arrive?

Treat the first preview, full edited gallery, teaser, main film and albums as separate milestones. Album production may also depend on your selection and approval.

For your proposal: The studio has not supplied confirmed delivery dates for this preview. Get each milestone and the update contact in your proposal.

What do RAW files and unedited JPEGs mean?

Camera RAW files are camera-source image files. JPEGs are exported or camera-processed image files. An unedited JPEG is not the same as a camera RAW file. The original package artwork’s phrase raw photos in JPEG needs clarification.

For your proposal: Confirm file formats, selected quantities and any extra charges before booking.

How are skin tones, outfit colours and retouching handled?

Look at complete examples in lighting similar to yours and explain the colours and finish you prefer. General colour adjustments and detailed skin retouching are different levels of editing.

For your proposal: Ask what editing is included, what requires approval and how extra retouching is charged.

Can we request changes to photographs or films?

Agree a feedback process, the number of revision rounds and the point at which an edit is considered approved. Give consolidated feedback so the team can address it clearly.

For your proposal: Included revisions, extra charges and deadlines need the studio’s confirmation.

Can we discuss the music and sound in our film?

Share music references, important voices and performances you want preserved. Ask how music is selected and how the final film will be used or shared.

How will we receive files and how long will they remain available?

Confirm the delivery method, download resolution, who can access the gallery and its expiry or retention period. Once delivered, keep your own copies of the files you want to preserve.

How do album selections and approvals work?

Agree who selects the photographs, the album specifications, how layout approval happens and when printing starts. A parent copy, extra pages and a separate design can affect the cost.
